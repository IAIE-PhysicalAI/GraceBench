Success is not stability. Two executions of the same task reach the goal: one stops after three actions; the other repeats a six-cycle loop. Follow the recorded evidence into a task-specific graph, step-level diagnosis, and full-trajectory metrics.

About the video, case validation, and source recordings

Coverage (0–8s) combines 12 recordings from ten apps. The case comparison (8–23s) shows all physical actions from two detailed-instruction Xiaohongshu-09 runs: Qwen3.8-27B (3 actions) and GPT-5.5 (15 actions). These are the same parameterized task with different live content, not a controlled head-to-head model ranking. Clips are action-centered and variably accelerated; the stable terminal frame is held. At 23s, one real loop is replayed at original speed, followed by graph alignment and calculations. The last 12 seconds animate all eleven models' detailed-instruction results from manuscript Table 2.

Every case action was visually checked against pre/post screenshots, temporal video evidence, and recorded coordinates. Both runs cover 3/3 required transitions. The unstable run has 6/15 deviations and 12/15 loop-covered actions. Its six one-step recoveries give RA=1; the stable run has no recovery opportunity, so RA is N/A. The original evaluator called the detour action “Back”; the actual click is the author entry. The film corrects that description without changing the validated state transitions or counts. The unstable agent reaches the goal at its action limit; it does not emit a clean completion signal.

Counts and metrics use the full trajectories, not edited excerpts. “ON / R” denotes an on-path action closing a recovery episode; loop membership is a separate sequence annotation. The full analysis contains 3,718 evaluated trajectories; 3,042 belong to the local video-validation population and 676 imported GUI-model records have no media. Abstract

Why execution stability?

A clean execution and an unstable execution can satisfy the same semantic milestones. Final success does not tell us whether the agent took a detour, repeated actions, or recovered from a mistake. In GraceBench, 51.24% of successful trajectories still contain a deviation or loop.

The GraceBench framework

GraceBench constructs UI Transition Graphs (UTGs), curates task-specific subgraphs with alternative valid routes, and evaluates agents on physical Android devices. Synchronized action logs, screenshots, and video clips map each interaction back to the graph.

Alongside success rate (SR) and completion rate (CR), the benchmark measures deviation rate (DR), loop burden rate (LBR), and recovery through recovery ability (RA), recovery success rate (RSR), and average recovery latency (ARL).

Experimental results

Eleven models are evaluated on all 169 tasks under detailed and concise instructions, with a maximum of 15 actions per run. GPT-5.6 Sol achieves the highest success rate in both settings, but the lowest recovery ability on its observed recovery opportunities.

Benchmark coverage

The task suite spans Douyin, Dianping, Douyin Mall, Fanqie Novel, Amap, Hongguo, Qishui Music, TikTok, Toutiao, and Xiaohongshu. Ten reviewed application-level UTGs contain 2,909 canonical GUI states and 3,116 observed transitions.

We plan to release task definitions, instruction variants, UTG metadata, evaluation code, metric definitions, and trace schemas. See the paper and appendix for the current protocol and human expert audit.
